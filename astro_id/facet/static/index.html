Astro

Notes

What Astro does

Astro plate-solves your night-sky photos: it matches the stars in each frame against a star catalog to work out exactly where the camera was pointing. From that it names what is in the frame (constellations, planets, the Moon, bright stars, Messier / NGC objects) and gives every name a confidence from 0 to 100.

The flow

  1. Load the photos (top of the panel). Open a photo or an album in Facet and click the button that names it, e.g. Load folder astro (18 photos). Or pick an Album, or Import a new folder by pasting its path. A folder or album you used before opens with its setup already applied, at the next useful step.
  2. Location (1), once per folder or album.
  3. Time (2): how far the camera clock was off. After a solve, Auto may offer a correction.
  4. Plate solve (3): one button. This only reads the photos; nothing in Facet changes.
  5. Results and tags (4): what was found in each photo. Untick photos you do not want, then Save to Facet writes the tags and the sky labels.
  6. Sky labels (5): open a photo in Facet and choose what is drawn over it.
  7. Export (6), optional: JPG copies with exactly those labels burned in, for sharing outside Facet.

There is no "save setup" button: the setup (location, time, JPG or RAW, the sliders, the export choices) is saved for the folder or album by itself whenever you change it and when you solve.

Words used here

Tag
A searchable word on a photo in Facet, e.g. cygnus or jupiter. You filter the gallery by tags. Written by Save to Facet.
Sky labels
The names Facet draws on top of the photo, each with a small circle at the object. Stored with the photo by Save to Facet, but drawn only while this panel is open and Show sky labels is on. They never change the photo file.
Labeled copies
Separate JPG files with the sky labels burned into the image, made by Export and written to a "<folder>-annotated" folder beside the originals. The originals are never changed.
Stick figures
The lines that join a constellation's main stars into its usual shape (and the shape of an asterism such as the Big Dipper).
Confidence
0 to 100: how sure Astro is that a name is right and visible in that frame. It combines how well the frame solved, how precisely the object's position is known at that spot in the frame, how bright it is compared with the faintest stars the photo caught, and whether it is cut by the frame edge or sits near the horizon. Rough guide: 80+ high, 65 to 79 likely, below 65 a candidate.
Grade
How well a whole frame solved (A best, C weakest), from the number of matched stars and how tightly they fit.

The two confidence sliders

They do different jobs:

  • Save names at confidence ≥ (step 4, default 65) decides what is saved. Names at or above it become tags. Raising it and saving again removes the tags that are now below it; lowering it adds them.
  • Show names at confidence ≥ (step 5) decides what is shown on the photo, and what Export burns into the copies. It is display only: moving it never changes tags. Every name found is stored as a sky label, so you can show more (or fewer) names than you tagged.

A star that belongs to a tagged constellation is not tagged on its own (the constellation tag covers it). It still appears as a sky label.

Setup details

Load what Facet shows
Facet tells the panel which album you are viewing, which photo is open, which photos are ticked, and which import opened the panel. The button offers the matching thing: the album, the open photo's folder, the ticked photos or the import. It does not see a folder you are only browsing: open one of its photos first.
Album or folder
Smart albums are not listed, because they are saved filters rather than fixed sets of photos; load their folder instead. Import a new folder loads a folder by its path; Facet must already have its photos (import or scan it in Facet first).
Location
Needed to place the planets and the Moon and to know where the horizon was. Use Google Earth or Google Maps, find the spot, copy its coordinates (or the page URL) and paste them, then Check and Confirm. Confirm writes the location to the photos in Facet and to the folder's setup. Photos that already carry camera GPS keep theirs unless you tick also replace GPS the camera recorded.
Time offset
How far the camera clock was off, e.g. +2559d11h08m. The ± minutes allow for not knowing it exactly: every time-sensitive check is tried across that whole window and the worst case is used, so a wide window lowers confidence for planets and objects near the horizon.
Auto (time)
Plate solving itself does not need the time: the stars fix the pointing on their own. The time only matters for the Moon, the planets and the horizon. After a solve, a frame where the Moon was found tells how far its position is from where your clock says it should be; Astro then offers the corrected offset with a ± that covers the spread between those frames. Planets and the horizon do not pin the time yet, so on a night without the Moon in a solved frame there is no Auto suggestion. Applying it changes the setup; solve again to use it.
JPG or RAW
Which file to solve from (under Photos). JPG is faster and usually enough.
Where setup is kept
In each folder's astro-id.json and, when you loaded an album, with that album too.

Export

Draws exactly what Sky labels (step 5) shows (the same groups, the same Show names at confidence and Max magnitude) on a copy of each photo. At export time you choose: Draw constellation stick figures, Show confidence numbers after each name, and an info box with the date, time and how well the frame solved. It uses the results already found (no new solve), or the sky labels stored in Facet when you solved in an earlier session.

Culling (optional)

Flags frames that look soft, trailed or have too few stars as review or reject. It never deletes or rejects anything in Facet. Apply tag adds an astro-review tag to those frames so you can find them; it works only on photos you have already saved to Facet.

Is it safe to repeat?

Yes. Solving and exporting write nothing to Facet. Saving again writes only what changed, and Astro only ever changes its own tags and sky labels, never the ones you or other tools added.
